# Experts judge influential assumptions in global irrigation models epistemically vulnerable

<!-- badges: start -->
<!-- badges: end -->

Seth N. Linga, Carmen Aguiló-Rivera, Olivia Richards, Samuel Flinders, Ana-Maria Creţu, Joshua Larsen, Michela Massimi, Giovanni De Grandis, José María García Avilés, and Arnald Puy

This is the GitHub repository of the paper, whose abstract is the following:

Computational models represent environmental systems using assumptions about which elements to include and how to represent them. Although uncertainty and sensitivity analyses can probe quantitative assumptions by varying them within plausible numerical ranges, many assumptions are qualitative or pragmatic and therefore escape traditional methods, leaving potential sources of uncertainty underexamined. We address this gap in global irrigation modelling through an expert elicitation workshop involving eleven scientists and six irrigators, who assessed the influence and pedigree (quality of the knowledge base) of key assumptions drawn from 51 studies. Scientists identified assumptions on irrigated area, irrigation efficiency and water availability, particularly the reliance on single datasets, as drivers of irrigation water withdrawals. Both scientists and irrigators judged these assumptions highly influential yet weak in pedigree, reflecting limited empirical support, practical constraints, multiple alternatives and low consensus. By examining how assumptions are produced and justified, we surface deep uncertainties in global water assessments.
